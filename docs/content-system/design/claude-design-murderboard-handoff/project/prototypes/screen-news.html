Local Illustrator's Estate Dispute Surfaces Amid Ongoing Investigation

By Renata Cho, Staff Writer · Published Tuesday, 6:00 AM

Court filings reviewed by the Ledger show that Dana Marsh, the 34-year-old illustrator found dead in Rittenhouse Square last week, had recently amended her will to remove a close associate as executor — a change her attorney says was finalized less than two weeks before her death.

"She wanted it settled quickly," said Ellen Kwan, the estate attorney who prepared the amendment, declining to name the associate being removed. "That's all I can say given the circumstances."

Neighbors have identified the associate in question as Marcus Vell, who lived in Marsh's building and employed her to walk his dog, Biscuit, six mornings a week. Vell has not responded to requests for comment. Building records show he was traveling the week of the incident, though hotel documentation obtained separately by this paper indicates his stay ended several hours earlier than he has publicly stated.

"Removing someone as an executor and revoking their access to a building are two completely different processes — one doesn't undo the other."

Legal experts note that estate changes and physical access — such as spare keys or door codes — are handled through entirely separate channels, meaning a falling-out reflected in a will might not be visible anywhere else on paper.

Police have not named a suspect. The investigation remains open.
