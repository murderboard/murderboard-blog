## Documents

### Marcus's logbook — the entry          %%id: doc-logbook%%

A battered composition book kept under the front desk for the three a.m. oddities. Between an item about the recycling and one about an idling truck: *"Prof H — man hanging around, past few days. Middle-aged. Didn't look lost. Keep an eye."* Dated ~10 days before the death. Photographed; the book stays at the desk.

### The Cornerstone money pattern — archive findings          %%id: doc-money%%

Fifty years of the Philadelphia Tribune, read in an afternoon. No single clipping says it. A 1962 profile mentions the label's *famously careful* artist contracts in passing. An estate dispute in the early seventies runs two items and evaporates. In 1978 a singer's widow sues over royalties; settled, terms undisclosed, *the catalog remains intact.* And the obituaries, all ending the same way: *his recordings remain in the Cornerstone catalog.* Bought once. Flat. Forever.

## Board Notes (meta)

Episode 7 gave the board its geometry — theft on the left with a name at the top, death on the right with nothing at the top at all. Episode 8 gives the right side a *weight* rather than a suspect: Cornerstone rises on motive alone, with Lena's own disclaimer attached.

**What isn't on this board is the point.** Lena stood across a street from an old man in a hat being helped down four steps and had nothing to write down — no name, no photograph, and no second face on Cornerstone's About page when she went looking. *Three generations* and two of them named. She let it go, because her theory is about the company, not the signature.

Also noticed and deliberately unpinned: the Saturday park man came from — or went toward — the east, which is where Cornerstone is. Lena raised it and took it apart herself; terror is the worst witness there is. It reassembled as direction, not evidence. And Margaret placing Reese fondly at the orchestra board, back in Episode 7, which Lena didn't react to at all.
